Vallam Lore Reference
Final Project Proposal

A reference site for an eldritch mystery campaign set on the world of Morphe, Anno 1926. Six pages, fifteen components, three weeks.

1. Topic

Starting fresh, with a new subject. Not a continuation of Project 1 and not the Missoula client site from Project 2. The subject is Vallam, a lore reference built from a 69-page setting document: roughly 22,000 words, 35 pieces of art, and one 5333×8000 city map.

Disclosure: a working prototype already exists

I built a version of this site earlier in the term before switching to the Missoula client project, so six pages already exist and all fourteen current components are built and proven. That is why the hour estimate in section 6 is low. The work ahead is improving a concept rather than discovering one, and I would rather state that plainly than present finished work as a plan.

2. Goal

To let someone find one specific piece of a 22,000-word world in seconds.

This is a reference, not a novel. The source material is a single long PDF, which is a fine way to write a setting and a useless way to run a session in one. Finding whether the Harwick-Sel family is allied to the Congregation means scrolling a document and hoping. The site's whole job is to make any faction, family, neighbourhood or piece of worldbuilding reachable without reading in order and without passing things nobody asked for.

The secondary goal is orientation: a new player should be able to understand the shape of the world in ten minutes without being handed 69 pages.

3. Who it serves, and what they need to do

Three visitors, with genuinely different needs. The site has to serve all three without making any of them pay for the others.

VisitorWhat they needWhat they do on the site
The newcomer Orientation. They do not yet know what questions to ask. Enter through one of five gateway cards, read the part introduction, follow the sticky rail down through the sections in order.
The returning player One named thing they half-remember, often by its emblem rather than its name. Go straight to Factions or Houses from the nav, scan the crest index, click through to that entry.
The GM, mid-session A single fact, in seconds, while four people wait. Look up a faction's seat and allegiance in its dossier, or find a neighbourhood by its map grid reference.

What all three need to do is arrive at a named entity in one or two clicks. That is the measure of whether this site works, and it is where the current build is weakest, which section 7 addresses.

The budget is exactly full

Fifteen of fifteen, with nothing spare. If a sixteenth becomes necessary I will consolidate rather than ask for an exception: .crest-index and .place-index are both grids of small uniform tiles and could become one .tile-index with modifiers, which frees a slot without changing a single page.

6. Time estimate

Using the formula from the brief: one hour per component already in the library, three hours per component built from scratch, totalled and divided across the three remaining weeks.

14 components already built and proven14 × 114
1 component from scratch (.entry-filter)1 × 33
Sum 17 hours
17 hours across 3 weeks 5.7 hours / week

I want to be honest about what this number is and is not. It is low because the components already exist, and the formula rewards that. It does not account for the content work of writing and entering the additional factions, for the accessibility and performance fixes in section 7, or for testing. My own estimate for the real total is closer to 28–32 hours, or about ten a week. The formula's value here is that it correctly identifies this as a project with very little construction risk and a lot of refinement work, which is the opposite of how Project 2 started.

7. Improving on the original concept

The brief asks for improvement rather than repetition. These are the specific things the prototype got wrong or left undone, each one measured.

ProblemMeasuredFixCosts a slot?
Nothing is searchable. With 48 entries and 82 sections, findability rests entirely on the rail and the two indexes. factions.html is about 18,000px tall .entry-filter Yes, the one free slot
The pages are heavy. Art was resized but never format-optimised. vallam.html 1.9 MB, index.html 858 KB, art folder 3.4 MB AVIF and WebP with JPEG fallback and srcset, reusing the pipeline built for Project 2, where AVIF came in near 40% of JPEG No, a build change
The home page has no headings. The five gateway names are styled spans, so a screen reader gets an h1 and then nothing. 0 h2 on index.html Promote the gateway names to real headings No, a markup fix
One element fails contrast. .crest-absent, the diamond shown where an organisation has no emblem. 2.46:1, against 4.5 required Raise its value, or replace it with a text label No
Typefaces load from a third party. Offline or blocked, the page falls back to Georgia and Gill Sans, which changes the look. 3 families from Google Fonts Self-host with font-display: swap No
Documentation has drifted. The old wireframes name components that were never built, and the README counts 10 neighbourhoods where the build has 9. 4 phantom components named Already corrected in the accompanying wireframes; README next No

8. Scope

In scope: six static pages generated from lore.json; fifteen components; the client-side filter; responsive layouts tested at 390px, 760px and 1400px; WCAG AA for every text and background pair; the ComponentLibrary entries.

Out of scope: accounts, comments, a backend or database, a content management system, a session tracker, player-editable content, and printing. None of these serve the goal in section 2, and the site is deliberately static so it can be hosted anywhere and opened at a table with no infrastructure behind it.

9. Risks

RiskLikelihoodResponse
The component cap. Any new idea in the next three weeks has nowhere to go. Moderate Consolidate .crest-index with .place-index before asking for an exception.
Content work expands. Writing new factions is open-ended in a way that building a component is not. High Cap the new entries at a fixed number in week 1 and treat anything beyond it as after the deadline. Content is the most common cause of a late project.
Art provenance. Every image needs to be my own or cleared. Low Audit the 51 files in week 1. This is the mistake Project 2 is still carrying and it will not be repeated here.
The filter is the only untested thing in the project. Low It is one component with no dependencies. If it fails, the rail and the indexes still work and the site ships without it.
